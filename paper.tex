\begin{proposition}[]\protect\hypertarget{prp-metric}{}\label{prp-metric}

Let \(x,y \in \Psi\) be statistical graphics. Let
\(g: \Psi \xrightarrow{} \mathbb{R}\) be the Euclidean distance between
the data of \(x\) and \(y\) before the aesthetic function, \(A\) is
applied, such that \(g(x,y) = d(A^{-1}(x), A^{-1}(y))\). Let
\(h: \Psi \xrightarrow{} \mathbb{R}\) be a piecewise distance function
that measures the human perception of statistical graphics, defined as:
\[
h(x, y) = \begin{cases} 
      g(x,y) & g(x,y) \geq \epsilon \\
      0 &  g(x,y) < \epsilon
   \end{cases}
\] where \(\epsilon\) is the JND of our human observer (with
\(\epsilon>0\)). \(h\) is not a metric.

\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Assume (\(\Psi\), \(h\)) is a metric space. Let \(a,b,c \in \Psi\) be
three different visualisations, where \(b\) is exactly halfway between
\(a\) and \(c\), such that \(g(a, c) =  \epsilon\),
\(g(a,b) = \frac {1}{2} \epsilon\), and
\(g(b, c) = \frac {1}{2} \epsilon\). Therefore, \(h(a, c) =  \epsilon\),
\(h(a,b) = 0\), and \(h(b, c) = 0\). As (\(\Psi\), \(h\)) is a metric
space, the triangular inequality should hold, and we can state
\[h(a, c) \leq h(a,b) + h(b, c)\] which implies \[0 \leq \epsilon\]
Therefore, by contradiction, \(h\) is not a metric.
\end{proof}
